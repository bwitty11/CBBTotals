How the model works

Baseline (log5-style possession model): Expected Tempo = (Home AdjTempo × Away AdjTempo) / League Avg Tempo. Team's expected efficiency = (Team AdjOE × Opponent AdjDE) / League Avg Efficiency. Team's baseline points = Expected Tempo / 100 × that efficiency.

Four Factors adjustment: each of eFG%, TOV rate, ORB rate, and FT rate gets the same log5-style matchup estimate, compared to league average, then combined into a multiplier (weighted by your Settings weights × the Sensitivity dial) that nudges each team's baseline points up or down. FF Adj shows how many points this layer added or subtracted.

Edge = Model Total − Market Total. Z-Score = Edge ÷ your backtested standard deviation of model error. Signal is categorized against your own Lean/Strong thresholds — not a recommendation from this tool.

AdjOE/AdjDE already reflect a team's season-average four factors performance, so this layer is meant to capture only the matchup-specific tilt on top of that — keep Sensitivity low until backtesting says otherwise, to avoid double-counting the same effect twice.

Why the default weights are eFG 0.30 / TOV 0.30 / ORB 0.25 / FTR 0.15 (not Dean Oliver's original 40/25/20/15): Oliver's split was an ad hoc estimate, not a fitted result, and several independent regressions against actual outcomes since then land closer to ~45% eFG / ~35% TOV / ~12% ORB / ~7% FTR — consistently shifting weight away from rebounding and free throw rate toward shooting and turnovers. On top of that, this tool's Four Factors layer sits on top of AdjOE/AdjDE, which are themselves built almost entirely from these same four ingredients — meaning eFG%, the biggest single driver of AdjOE, is also the factor most redundant with it. So the weight here is trimmed below even what those regressions suggest, and shifted toward TOV/ORB, which are more likely to reflect a genuine head-to-head stylistic mismatch (pressure defense vs. shaky ball handling, elite rebounding vs. a small lineup) that a single aggregate efficiency number can miss. FT rate stays modest either way, since foul rates are officiating-dependent and noisier game-to-game than a stable team skill. Sensitivity was nudged from 0.35 to 0.40 for the same reason — less weight now sits on the most-redundant factor, so the overall layer carries a bit less double-counting risk than before. None of this is proven for college hoops specifically (the regressions above are all NBA data, and college has more variance in rebounding schemes) — treat it as a reasoned starting point, not a final answer, and use the Backtest tab below once you have enough completed games to check it.

Informational tool only — not betting advice. Sports betting carries variance and risk of loss; the market total already reflects a lot of information, so a model edge is not a guarantee.
